EO 263's implementation rules were issued through DAO 96-291o and revised in 2004 through DAO 2004-29".

CBFM banners the phrase "people first and sustainable forests shall follow". While the core attention of CBFM is on people, actions are geared towards making the environment healthy and beneficial to the present and future generations. It is anchored on the premise that if local communities are given access to and control over forest resources and are allowed to benefit from them, they become more responsible stewards and partners in promoting sustainable forest management. CBFMA and other tenure instruments like PACBRMA intend to provide holders with that bundle of rights over forest resources (within multiple-use and buffer zones for PACBRMA holders).

## The Land Tenure Instruments

Through CBFM, organized communities, also called "Forest Managers", are granted access to forest resources under long-term tenure provided they use environment-friendly, ecologically-sustainable and labor-intensive management and harvesting methods. These are in the form of land tenure instruments (LTI) like Community-Based Forest Management Agreement (CBFMA) for forestland communities; Certificate of Ancestral Do i   o  o  ooiea Community-based Resource Management Agreement (PACBRMA) for communities in legislated and executive-proclaimed Protected Areas (PA).

While CADTs entitle IPs ownership of their ancestral domains, CBFMAs and PACBRMAs grant rights to communities to manage, develop and utilize forest areas for livelihood for a period of 25 years. CBFMAs and PACBRMAs are renewable for another 25 years, while CADTs grant eternal ownerships. All tenure instruments bear the holders' responsibilities to rehabilitate, conserve, and protect the areas awarded to them, and the resources within.

## CBFM ACHIEVEMENTS, CONTRIBUTIONS AND CHALLENGES

CBFM, as DENR's banner approach in people-oriented forest management, has proven that it truly has contributed in sustaining forest ecosystems, creating better economic opportunities, and developing local leaders and the culture of good governance. Third-party assessments of CBFM, DENR-led annual assessment workshops and documentations, field monitoring reports, and researches show that there had been positive changes in the last 27 years. But while progress is manifested, CBFM also faced various challenges that restricted a more efficient advancement.

## Achievements and Contributions

1. Obvious biophysical improvement in many CBFM areas. The CBFM-POs' projects with National Greening Program (NGP), CBFM-Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Project (CBFM-CARP) that centered on agroforestry development, plantation establishment, mangrove/forest rehabilitation, and livelihood management, and special projects and contracts largely contributed to this development. Some CBFM-POs and their members have maintained and/or reaped from their plantations that were established through the reforestation programs under the ADB Loan in the 1980s.
2. CBFM contributes to mitigating forest fragmentation and degradation. People's dependence on the land and other resources in the forests has largely contributed to the value and discipline that farming families or households put on the piece of land each of them till. ## 3. Livelihood and enterprise development

It is essential to establish sustainable, environmentally-sound, green and biodiversity-friendly enterprises in order to manage and maintain forests and reduce poverty among (forest) communities. It is crucial that livelihoods and enterprises are carefully selected, designed, established, and managed to generate income without harming the environment.

Rehabilitation and area development projects implemented in CBFM areas in the last decade or so have led to areas with now harvestable

In the next 10 years and beyond, CBFM-POs, their members, and their communities shall be expected to maintain natural resource-based livelihoods and encouraged to put up enterprises. This plan shall also push forward present enterprises in CBFM communities, expand, scaleup and add value to products in order to be integrated with the value chain.

The development of highly-competitive enterprises will always need sufficient data and analysis of community resources, market, and potential products or business. At production level, CBFM entrepreneurs need to have quality control system, good packaging and promotional strategies, business management systems, and Lus l mo mo o om subsistence level to market-oriented, and highly-competitive, economically-viable enterprises. communitylevel.

Support mechanisms like secured tenure, capacity-building, and policies favorable to CBFM entrepreneurs (see discussion on policies under strategy #5) should be in place to hasten the development of livelihoods and enterprises for the communities. There should be an intentional plan to take advantage of the opportunities posed by carbon trading in the international market, which can be a source of income for the community.

Resources, capitalization, technologies, and technical assistance shall be made more accessible to CBFM-POs, their members and their communities. CBFM-POs are also encouraged to roll income from their enterprises and savings from projects to ensure continuity of enterprise initiatives. This way, CBFM-POs become more independent from external sources and become more empowered and resilient.
